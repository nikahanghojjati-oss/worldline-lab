## R005 new software experiment: decompose, analogize, compose, attack, iterate

HAMMER's `assets/hammer-discovery.js` validates four hypotheses in `data/hammer.json`, then evaluates four authored scientific missions and automatically generates additional combinations with a two-level, bounded beam search. The program:

1. **Decomposes** each hypothesis into specified rival models, a set of outcomes, nuisance variants, evidence class and impact scope.
2. **Types** candidate mixtures: physical rival identities and scope must agree. It vetoes attempts to treat a measurement constraining *collapse dynamics* as direct evidence that standard *Bohmian configuration* is false.
3. **Combines** compatible instruments into a conditional joint experiment with `p(x,y|H,η_x,η_y)=p(x|H,η_x)·p(y|H,η_y)`. This is a declared **independence hypothesis** for a design exercise, not a fact about the apparatus.
4. **Attacks** every joint proposal with the finite adversarial nuisance set. It searches for the minimum Jensen–Shannon distance between specified model distributions. One detector-drift alternative may defeat it; model classes and uncertainty bounds are not calibrated here.
5. **Iterates** the best bounded candidates into another round; negative, no-go and category-veto traces stay as explicit research memory. The next experiment question is based on the reason the previous test failed, not merely generated marketing language.

**Four authored missions:**

- HF-001: repeated standard Born pointer statistics. Correct result: no distinguishable Everett/Bohm branch-count observation under matched product-form standard quantum measurement assumptions.
- HF-002: recovery test + reference arm, showing a *conditional* design wedge for two **invented** unitary-vs-loss probability families. Strong shared-detector correlation could invalidate the product assumption.
- HF-003: ontology/collapse category veto. Mathematical noncompatibility prevents false transfer of evidence about model dynamics to physical branch counts.
- HF-004: repeated recovery-arm design, conditional on stable independent preparation; identifies correlated drift as a falsifier.

**Computational gates:** deterministic normalization; canonical model IDs; bounded outcomes, nuisance variants, beam width and recursion depth; prior/source trace; repeated-Born no-go; conditional dynamics wedge; invalid-fusion veto; score remains neutral 50.

## R005 mathematical counterexample: where a deeper question emerges

Suppose two hypothetical joint distributions across two binary records are

```text
            00    01    10    11
Model P    0.5   0.0   0.0   0.5
Model Q    0.0   0.5   0.5   0.0
```

Both models' *individual-record* marginals are exactly `(0.5, 0.5)` for each bit, so a single-record JSD is **zero**. But the full-joint JSD is **one bit**, since their supports are disjoint. This demonstrates that **equality of individual marginals does not imply equality of joint correlations**.

**Crucial anti-hallucination guardrail:** This is **not** a discovery that standard Everett and standard quantum-equilibrium Bohm make different joint predictions. R012 already derives matched full quantum-instrument outcome equality, including sequences in its domain. To turn the correlated-outcomes idea into a test capable of addressing *actual physical histories*, specify an extended rival theory with a physically motivated joint prediction, real feasible correlated measurement protocol, calibrated nuisance model, independent source provenance, statistical uncertainty and actual relevant data. Without those, it is a creative **research direction**, not evidence favoring one or many.

The second firewall is classical postprocessing: if full operational `p=q`, applying the **same** stochastic channel `T` yields `Tp=Tq`; measurement reporting/merging/copying cannot manufacture discrimination. `assets/hammer-discovery.js` implements and tests this restricted fact. The result is elementary probability theory, not an original proof of all quantum interpretations' equivalence.
