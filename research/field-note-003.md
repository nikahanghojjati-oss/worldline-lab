## Question and precise conclusion
Does quantum field theory prevent construction of all time machines? **Not presently established.** Hawking (1992) gives a conditional, influential chronology-protection argument; Kay–Radzikowski–Wald (1997) prove strong quantum-field-theoretic obstructions at base points of *compactly generated* Cauchy horizons; Visser (1997) argues the semiclassical framework used to reason about backreaction may become physically unreliable before the chronology horizon. Each statement is more precise than either “time travel proven possible” or “time travel proven impossible.”

## Technical reconciliation of the apparent disagreement
KRW §5 p.27–28 compares local Hadamard singularity structure against a two-point function propagated along a globally returning null geodesic. Even points that are locally spacelike separated may be connected by a global null path. Singularities propagate along that global null path; a locally constructed Hadamard subtraction lacks the matching singularity, yielding a mismatch. The proof depends on microlocal propagation-of-singularities theorems, whose correctness is *not independently reconstructed here*. A finite limit of the mean stress tensor along selected approaches need not repair local Hadamard regularity *at* a base point. This avoids a common but invalid claim that the theorem proves an infinite mean energy everywhere prior to chronology violation.

Visser §§II–V defines an unreliable region using Planck-short nontrivial closed spacelike geodesics (with refinements for winding and curvature) and its causal future boundary as the reliability horizon. His warning is epistemic: predictions of the semiclassical theory may no longer be trustworthy there, even when mean stress backreaction is weak. This is compatible with KRW identifying a mathematical breakdown of a conventional quantum field description, but it **does not identify how full quantum gravity resolves the spacetime**. In fact the theories can concur that the approximation breaks, yet still disagree on the physical mechanism and ultimate outcome.

A 1997 Roman-ring conditional geometry offers an additional stress-test of any *universal* claim that the mean stress must always become huge before the reliability boundary. As only its publisher abstract was checked, this example is a candidate counterargument pending detailed derivation review, not independently certified.
