## SRC-075 · Valentini and Varma (2025), *Toward a test of the Born rule in high-energy collisions*

Primary complete author text: https://arxiv.org/html/2505.07510 (arXiv 2505.07510v4, 28 December 2025); publisher identity Phys. Rev. D 112, 112024, 30 December 2025, https://doi.org/10.1103/4llk-pt1j. All six sections examined: §1 scope/assumptions; §2 two-state Born/Bell-model mathematics; §3 top-quark and neutral-pion photon sources plus polarimetry event/noise/angle budgets; §4 tau helicity and collider observables; §5 Born-dependent MC event selection; §6 limitations and proposals, figures 1–7 and cited experimental inputs. Bibliography surveyed for contrary mechanisms; individual citations not independently read in full during this round.

- **Explicit candidate distinction:** a *specified* pilot-wave nonequilibrium distribution over hidden variables can yield $E(\mathbf m)$ nonlinear in spin/polarization axis, e.g. cubic correction $R$ to Malus law. Standard Everett and quantum-equilibrium Bohmian mechanics predict ordinary Born expectations. This is an experimentally discriminable added physical premise, **not** a demonstration that every Bohmian model differs from Everett or that a deviation would uniquely identify Bohm.
- **Observable and method:** §2 eqs. 4–31 derive $p^+(\Theta;R)=\cos^2\Theta+(R/2)f(\Theta)$, with $f(\Theta)=-\cos(2\Theta)\sin^2(2\Theta)$. Compare counts at prespecified angles; the simple Bell hidden-vector model has constraints at 0, 45, and 90 degrees. Photons from top production and neutral pion decay, or ensemble tau-lepton helicity from Z bosons, are proposed experimental channels.
- **Independent algebra check:** §3.3 eqs. 40–42 give $N_{min}=36/(\delta^2\sin^2(4\Theta))$. At $\Theta=\pi/8$ and $\delta=0.01$, $N_{min}=360,000$ background-free polarization-resolved photons for an approximate $3\sigma$ test. With signal fraction $f_s=0.8$, eq. 45 gives $360,000/0.8^2=562,500$ total events. §3.3 eq. 51 then gives a maximum ideal count bias of approximately 212 at 360,000 events. This is an analytical reproduction of printed prospective sample-size assumptions, not a test result or independently validated achievable detector precision.
- **Experimental feasibility:** §3.1 reports roughly 4,899 directly top-radiated photon events from a specific ATLAS analysis (5,734 total events split with decay-product radiative contributions), much less than the prospective polarization-resolved target; §3.2 reports about 200,000 RHICf Type-I pion decays, corresponding to about 400,000 photons, but present high-energy photon polarimetry cannot individually resolve the requisite polarization outcomes with controlled precision. Numbers come from papers the authors cite, not independent detector reprocessing.
- **Measured tau data are null-compatible:** §4.4 eqs. 70–73 quote expected mean helicity about -0.1472 ± 0.0016 versus CMS -0.144 ± 0.015, corresponding to $\epsilon\approx+0.0032$, uncertainty about $0.01509$, deviation only $0.21\sigma$ under their simplified comparison. This uses an *existing* tau helicity measurement repurposed by the authors; it is not a 0.21-sigma likelihood comparison between entire many-history and one-history ontologies.
- **Crucial adverse control:** §5 explicitly admits background simulation and event selection are trained using amplitudes squared, often the very Born assumption under test. Their provisional working assumption is that possible two-state spin/polarization deviations do not contaminate multiparticle scattering/modelled selection in the same way. A robust experiment must independently calibrate selection, acceptance, misidentification, energy and angular effects; a polarization anomaly could instead reflect detector response, event-model inadequacy or other beyond-Standard-Model dynamics.
- **Conclusion and score effect:** direct main-question discriminator *in principle* between a specified nonlinear Born-breaking $B_{neq}(R)$ and unitary Born models; **no established violation** and no empirical ontology movement. A targeted test needs a physical polarization analyzer, preregistered $R$, independent detector controls, and rigorous likelihoods rather than a single fitted curve. Appendix-level, CMS raw event files, polarimeter hardware trials, and independent reconstruction of the entire HEPData signal chain were not performed.

## SRC-076 · Travis Norsen (2018), *On the Explanation of Born-Rule Statistics in the de Broglie-Bohm Pilot-Wave Theory*

Complete open source https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7512940/ ; publisher https://doi.org/10.3390/e20060422, Entropy 20(6), 422. Main text sections §1 Introduction; §2 pilot wave continuity/measurement; §3 dynamical relaxation and H function; §4 mathematical and physical objections; §5 typicality foundations; §6 circularity objections; §7 proposed synthesis and conclusion; mathematical examples, figures 1–5 inspected in the text. Note: this is a *critical theoretical analysis* with illustrative numerical trajectories, not an independently observed test of parallel histories.

- **Core physics:** wavefunction $\Psi$ and actual configuration $Q$ evolve under unitary Schrödinger dynamics and guidance velocity $v=j/|\Psi|^2$ (§2 eqs. 5–9). When the ensemble configuration distribution equals $|\Psi|^2$, equivariance plus disjoint pointer branches gives ordinary Born statistics for *one actual pointer position*. In Everett, a unitary amplitude decomposition with the Born weight rule gives the same detector frequencies while treating branch records as real. Thus ordinary measurements do not prefer Everett versus equilibrium Bohm.
- **Competing internal explanations:** the dynamical relaxation approach (§3) studies coarse-grained approach of nonequilibrium initial distributions toward $|\Psi|^2$ via mixing; the typicality approach (§5) uses an equivariant equilibrium measure on universal configuration space to explain subsystem records. In either, the status of the measure and the actual universe need careful interpretation.
- **Adversarial limitations:** §4 notes nonrelaxing special configurations, no-fine-grained-microstructure initial assumption and the limitation that fine-grained entropy does not decrease. §6 notes that choosing $|\Psi|^2$ as typicality measure is contestable/could seem circular. §7 argues typicality and dynamical relaxation can inform one another, but the combined story does not prove all imaginable nonequilibrium initial configurations relax, nor demonstrate observed primordial disequilibrium.
- **Counterweights:** (1) ordinary Born confirmation alone provides no evidence that all branches are actual, because pilot-wave equilibrium matches it; (2) proposed nonequilibrium anomalies remain conditional on an initial distribution or physical regularization that actually generates one; (3) philosophically distinct accounts of probabilities should not be numerically scored from equal outcomes.
- **Source-depth limits:** Full main article read via HTML; original simulations described by Norsen and Valentini/Westman were *not* rerun; external DGZ arguments and competing Everett probability derivations were not independently proven. No new detection of $\rho\neq|\Psi|^2$.

## Matched-history pointer theorem and scope

For a normalized post-measurement entangled state $\Psi(q,y)=\sum_k c_k\,\psi_k(q)\Phi_k(y)$ with macroscopically disjoint apparatus pointer supports $Y_k$, Born unitary theory assigns $P_E(k)=|c_k|^2$ (assuming the operational branch-weight rule). Equilibrium Bohm assigns configurations $(Q,Y)$ density $|\Psi(q,y)|^2$, hence $P_{B,eq}(k)=\int_{Y_k}dy\int dq\,|\Psi(q,y)|^2\approx |c_k|^2$. Under exact disjoint supports and normalized branch states, equality is exact. With overlapping pointers / apparatus inefficiencies, use the *same* POVM or physical apparatus response in both models, so experimentally accessible outcome distributions still match under standard equilibrium assumptions.

For independent preparations with recorded outcomes $k_1,\dots,k_N$, the likelihood ratio $\prod_i P_E(k_i)/P_{B,eq}(k_i)=1$ wherever the common probabilities are nonzero. This proves lack of discrimination in the *matched Born-rule instrument class*, not a general proof about which ontology is real. An anomaly compared with *both* predictions can update a specified nonequilibrium or collapse model but does not automatically select a unique account of physically real histories.
