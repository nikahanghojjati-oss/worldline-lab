Breaking the Glass

01 / THE IMPOSSIBLE-LOOKING QUESTION

What would really break the glass?

Worldline Lab asks whether our universe has one actually realized history or multiple physically real histories. A quantum experiment can return an extraordinary result without answering that question. The obstacle is identifiability: if two physical theories predict the same distribution of every record we can collect in an experiment, better measurement precision cannot tell them apart.

BIG R · SCIENTIFIC LEAD

Different pictures of reality can agree on all the visible outcomes. HAMMER should isolate which physical assumptions create that agreement and develop tests that make competing models disagree. A surprising signal is only the beginning.

JE · ADVERSARIAL RESPONSE

I agree—and the toughest counterexample is quantum-equilibrium Bohmian mechanics. In their usual shared quantum-measurement domain, it predicts the same Born statistics as standard Everettian quantum mechanics. If both assign the same likelihood to our result, those data alone cannot decide how many histories are real.

02 / THE AGREEMENT

Two gates, one honest scientific program

GATE 01 · PHYSICAL REALITY

Establish the effect.

Reproduce an original analysis, quantify detector calibration and uncertainty, and rule out ordinary instrumental or modeling explanations. Or derive an auditable constraint on a specified alternative law. A model-specific upper limit counts as a result of that kind—not proof of multiple worlds.

GATE 02 · ONTOLOGY

Make histories disagree.

Define one-history and many-history rivals on the same event space. Derive different, testable outcome likelihoods and evaluate surviving countermodels. If Everett and equilibrium Bohm remain observationally equivalent, report that limit explicitly rather than translating a different discovery into a verdict on histories.

Big R accepted JE's two-gate approach and its adversarial safeguards; JE accepted Big R's scientific leadership and the joint strategy. The goal is not to win an internal debate. It is to build a test that survives one.

03 / THE FIRST HAMMER BLOW

A collider result—and a crucial doubt

A July 2026 analysis by Antony Valentini and Mira Varma revisited electron–positron Bhabha scattering measurements from SLAC's HRS detector at a center-of-mass energy of 29 GeV. The authors fitted a particular Gaussian angular-smearing model to 22 angular bins and reported a 95% upper limit of ε < 0.042. The fitted width ε = 0.024 is not a detection of a Born-rule violation.

JE · THE COUNTERATTACK

Before interpreting a fitted width as new physics, reproduce the calculation with exact finite-bin integration. A bin of width 0.05 in cos θ has a leading-order Gaussian-equivalent smoothing scale 0.05/√12 ≈ 0.0144 for smooth distributions. That is a mathematical analogy—not a correction to subtract from the published 0.024. Independently verify angular response, normalization, acceptance and correlated uncertainties.

BIG R · THE RESPONSE

Agreed, with a safeguard: bin averaging does not establish that the published signal is instrumental. The authors' actual fitting procedure and detector resolution must be reconstructed first, or we risk correcting the same effect twice. In an idealized case where physical and detector Gaussian smearing act on the same coordinate, only their combined variance is identified unless independent calibration breaks the degeneracy.

The controlled next comparison is unsmeared Standard Model theory integrated over the published angular bins versus the same properly integrated theory after an explicitly specified Gaussian deformation, with identical, source-grounded nuisance treatment. Actual event-level data have not been independently reproduced by Worldline Lab in this dialogue.

What can count after audit? A reproducible real-data fit, a calibrated nuisance exclusion, a validated model-specific physical constraint, or a rigorous proof that a proposed experiment cannot discriminate the desired ontologies. A completed non-identifiability proof is worthwhile scientific work; it is not evidence for one history or many.

Separate instrument: the Truth Compass remains at its neutral editorial anchor, 50/100. The value is not a measured 50% probability. GLASS points cannot transfer into it. The central assessment only changes when genuine, qualified observations differentiate competing physical-history predictions.

05 / WHAT HAPPENS NEXT

The experiment gets the last word.

  1. Recover the original source inputs. Verify the HRS table, error treatment and exact model equations. Report inaccessible inputs honestly.
  2. Run both finite-bin hypotheses. Reproduce the parameter profile, uncertainty coverage and instrument-response alternatives before judging any deviation.
  3. Try to defeat the result. Compare nuisance assumptions, binning decisions and physically distinct alternative mechanisms on the same measured events.
  4. Audit the history question separately. Keep Everett and equilibrium Bohmian mechanics in the rival set; pursue independently specified collapse/recoherence tests without mistaking a collapse-rate limit for a branch count.
  5. Publish the limitation as carefully as the result. Record a reproducible finding, a failure to reproduce, or a clearly scoped non-identifiability conclusion. No manufactured score movement.
BIG R & JE · UNIFIED CLOSING

Force a measurable disagreement, attack every rival explanation, and let nature—not a progress meter—settle the question.
